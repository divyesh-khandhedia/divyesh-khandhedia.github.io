Per-unit conversion

Change the voltage and MVA base of a three-phase impedance.

Method and assumptions

Zbase = kV²/MVA. Multiply each original pu impedance component by (MVAnew/MVAold) × (kVold/kVnew)². Select line-to-line voltage on the same physical side of the equipment.

Symmetrical components

Convert between phase and sequence phasors.

Enter A, B, C for phase inputs or 0, 1, 2 for sequence inputs, in that order.

Method and assumptions

Uses the Fortescue transform with ABC rotation and a = 1∠120°. All magnitudes use the same unit. Unbalance is 100 × |X₂|/|X₁| and is undefined when positive sequence is zero.

Sequence-network fault current

Calculate initial symmetrical RMS currents from a single-bus Thevenin equivalent.

Method and assumptions

Uses standard series and parallel sequence-network connections. Fault impedance is per phase for a balanced fault, between phases for B–C, or the common ground path for faults to ground. Use positive, negative and zero sequence impedances on the same base. Results exclude DC offset and equipment duty checks.

Two-bus PV curve

Find the constant-power-factor loadability limit and plot both voltage solutions.

Method and assumptions

Models a fixed source and lumped series impedance feeding a constant-PQ load. At each P, Q = P × Q/P. The quadratic in |V|² has two roots up to the nose point. Generator reactive limits, shunts and controls are outside this model.

Frequency nadir and RoCoF

Simulate aggregate primary frequency response after a generation loss.

Method and assumptions

Integrates 2H × dΔf/dt = ΔPm − ΔP − DΔf and Tg × dΔPm/dt = −Δf/R − ΔPm in per unit on the system base. This single-area screening model assumes unsaturated primary response, with no deadband, reserve limit, secondary control or UFLS.

IEC IDMT relay timing

Compare two inverse-time curves at the same primary fault current.

Method and assumptions

IEC inverse-time equation t = TMS × k / [(I/Iₛ)^α − 1]. The reported margin is upstream minus downstream operate time at this one current. Breaker clearing time, relay tolerances, instantaneous elements and coordination across fault cases are separate checks.

Transformer impedance split

Estimate leakage R and X from nameplate impedance and rated-current load losses.

Method and assumptions

R% = 100 × load loss in kW / rated kVA and X% = √(Z%² − R%²). Use the rated-current load loss at the test reference temperature, excluding no-load loss. Ohms are referred to the selected winding.

Cable DC resistance at temperature

Correct a manufacturer’s 20 °C resistance to the specified conductor temperature.

Method and assumptions

R(T)/R(20) = (K + T)/(K + 20), with K = 234.5 °C for copper or 228 °C for aluminium. Temperature is the conductor temperature. The estimate covers DC resistance per phase in one direction. AC skin and proximity effects require separate treatment.

Merit-order dispatch

Allocate generation to meet demand with constant marginal costs and an already committed fleet.

Method and assumptions

Start at each unit’s minimum output, then allocate remaining MW in ascending marginal-cost order up to each maximum. The objective is energy cost per hour. The model omits network constraints, losses, ramping, reserves and unit commitment.

SRF-PLL tuning

Estimate gains for a normalized synchronous-reference-frame PLL.

Method and assumptions

Kp = 2ζωn and Ki = ωn² for a normalized phase detector and a continuous-time PI loop. Natural frequency is the design input. Sampling, current-loop coupling and grid impedance are outside this model.

Grounding conductor thermal sizing

Estimate conductor area for a short-duration fault.

Method and assumptions

Uses S = I√t/k. Obtain k for the actual conductor and initial and final temperatures. Use current in the conductor under study. Mechanical minimum, touch and step voltage, and grounding grid design are separate checks.

Transformer losses and efficiency

Estimate losses at a given loading and power factor.

Method and assumptions

Uses constant no-load loss and load loss proportional to current squared at nominal voltage. Power factor is a magnitude. Use test losses at the applicable reference temperature.

Technical reference

Generator electrical conversions

Convert balanced three-phase current, kVA, kW and kVAr from a kW rating.

Method and assumptions

S = P/PF and I = 1000S/(√3V). Reactive power is reported as a magnitude. The result covers steady-state balanced loading. Generator sizing also requires load steps and site derating.

Technical reference

SCADA analog scaling

Convert raw counts or analog signals to engineering units.

Method and assumptions

Uses linear interpolation and extrapolates outside the raw range. Reversed engineering ranges are allowed.

Technical reference

Cable voltage drop

Estimate balanced three-phase voltage drop from cable resistance and reactance.

Method and assumptions

Approximate longitudinal drop √3 × I × L × (R cosφ + X sinφ). Positive power factor input denotes lagging load. Negative denotes leading with the same active power factor magnitude. Negative drop denotes voltage rise.
